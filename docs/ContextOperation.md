# PART I – Context and Operation

To develop the project, students must form groups/work teams common to all course units. The goal is to obtain an integrated solution, that is, a single system encompassing the various components / applications developed.<br><br>

The application of these best practices, techniques and technologies is an integral part of the final project assessment criteria. ### 2.1. Team Composition
The project is envisioned to be carried out by teams of **4 students**, who must remain the **same across all UCs** and preferably all students belonging to the same lab (PL) class (Regarding GESTA, the students must be of the same practical (TP) class.). Each team operates as it belongs to a company that is competing against the others to develop the desired system. **Each team's focus should always be on the project as a whole and not on the scope of each UC individually.**<br><br>
**Ideally, all team members (students) should be enrolled in all UC (see Table 3).** However, given the diversity of LEI students, other team compositions may be acceptable, as long as each course unit has at least three students enrolled (cf. Table 4 example).

### 2.2. Team Working Methodology
At the beginning of each sprint, a sprint backlog is provided. It consists of a set of user stories specifying the work to be performed by the team during the sprint. Then, the team is responsible for (per sprint):
1. analyzing this information and, if necessary, **requesting clarification in a timely manner for the successful completion of these user stories**;
2. **correctly applying the engineering process**, particularly the software development process and its various activities (e.g., analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment), **leaving evidence of its practice**;
3. **planning the tasks** to be performed considering both the sprint objectives and the existence of possible dependencies between tasks and, whenever necessary, reviewing and modifying the previously prepared plan;
4. **ensuring a balanced division of tasks and responsibilities among all team members**(e.g., in terms of effort, knowledge, and complexity) such that **any team member is able to describe and explain how a given requirement was/is implemented/met in the system and, if requested, is also capable of adding improvements/changes (e.g., when conducting an assessment).**

<u>Be aware that it is neither intended nor acceptable having any team member dedicate themselves solely to tasks that require the application of knowledge from a single course unit (e.g., ARQSI or SGRAI only). Rather, each team member should participate in tasks that require the application of knowledge from all courses units in which they are enrolled.</u><br><br>
It is also important emphasizing that everyone is responsible for ensuring the smooth running of the team. In the event of conflicts, the team should first attempt to resolve the conflict within the team and as a team. If this is not enough to solve the issue, the team should report it to the faculty responsible for monitoring the team.<br><br>
Finally, it is recommended that the team work regularly (e.g., daily, weekly) on the project and adopt appropriate tools, processes, and methods for effective project management, collaboration and execution, as well as for sharing and communicating information among all team members.

### 2.3. Project Assessment
At the end of each sprint, each participating course unit will conduct an individual and/or team assessment (formative and/or summative) of the work completed so far. **The assessment criteria are defined exclusively by each course unit and generally focus on the technical aspects of the project that fit within its syllabus. Therefore, the criteria applied vary and differ from one course unit to another.** The results (grades) obtained in one course unit do not directly influence the results of the other course units. Therefore, it is extremely important that students apply an appropriate work methodology and, in particular, regarding the tasks assignment (see section 2.2) to prevent any undesirable consequences (e.g., failure to achieve the objectives in a given course unit).<br><br>
At last, it should be notice that every course unit assessment will be performed in accordance with what is established in the respective Course Syllabus (FUC) and may include other assessments than the ones upcoming from the project development. In case of a given UC having additional assessments, those will tend to occur before the UC beginning its participation in the project or after the project development has ended (i.e., during the examination period).
